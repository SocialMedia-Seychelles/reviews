What customers see today

Right now, your website turns people away

Anyone who looks up homemakersseychelles.com on a phone gets this screen before they see a single product.

Why it happens

  • The security certificate ran out on 26 March 2026. Since then, Chrome, Safari and Edge warn visitors that the site "might be trying to steal your information", and offer a big "Back to safety" button.
  • Many people stop right there. A customer who was ready to come in for tiles or a fridge goes back to Google, where other stores are one tap away.
  • Behind the warning, the site has aged. The contact box still holds details from the original template (a New York address), and the footer says © 2022.

The good news: none of this says anything about the store. It's a website that needs replacing, and the replacement is already built.

Why a website matters for Homemakers

What a good site does for a home and building store

People in Seychelles plan a build or a new kitchen over weeks, mostly on their phones and mostly in the evening. A website is where that planning can lead to your door.

Open all night

Your store works after 6pm

Customers browse departments, make a list and send it at 10 at night. Your team finds it in the inbox in the morning, ready to price.

Found first

Where people check before they drive

"Hardware store Providence", "tiles Mahé", "is Homemakers open Saturday". A secure, up-to-date site gives Google something good to show, with your hours, directions and phone numbers.

Orders in writing

Lists instead of phone tag

Builders tap what they need (timber sizes, cement, tiles) and email the list to Providence or Victoria with their name and number. Fewer missed calls and fewer "what did they say they wanted?"

Trust

Confidence for bigger purchases

Before spending on a sofa, a bathroom suite or a roof's worth of timber, people check the business looks established. A modern, secure site says you're here, open and serious.

Brands you carry

Show off the names people ask for

Luminarc, Ronseal, Indesit, Alcolin. When customers can see the brands in each department, they know the trip is worth it.

Two stores, one site

Send people to the right branch

Each store has its own directions, tap-to-call buttons and an "open now / closed" sign that updates itself. Victoria for kitchenware, Providence for the big stuff.

Your own address

Not a listing next to competitors

On directory sites, your page sits beside other hardware stores. On your own site, every click stays with Homemakers.

Works with social

Somewhere to send everyone

Facebook posts, WhatsApp chats and Google Maps can all link to one place that shows the whole store, so every post and conversation has somewhere to lead.

The new site

Scroll and the house builds itself

Homemakers has everything a house needs, from the cement to the cushions. So the site is a house: as you scroll it goes from floor plan to foundations, frame and roof, walls, bathroom, furniture, kitchen and garden. At each stage, gold points open the departments for that part of the build.

The full site is live for you to try. Open it on your phone, scroll through, tap the departments and make a list. It's on a private preview address and isn't listed on Google.

Open the live site

Every department, one tap away

All 14 departments are on the house and in the menu: building materials, timber, tiles, paint, bathroom, plumbing and electrical, furniture, mattresses, appliances, kitchenware, plastic ware, household, garden and tools.

Each one opens with a photo and the things people come in for. Tap an item and it goes on the list.

The list goes straight to the store

Customers add quantities, a note ("4 m lengths, delivery to Anse Royale"), pick Providence or Victoria and send. It arrives as a normal email. There's no online payment and nothing for your team to manage: you reply with prices and stock as you do now.

Made to be remembered

Most store websites in Seychelles are a page of text and a contact form. This one is something people show each other. It's the kind of site that gets shared in a WhatsApp group when someone asks "where do I get tiles?"

A clear menu stays at the top the whole time, so anyone in a hurry can skip the house and go straight to a department, the stores or the phone numbers.

On phones, the text sits in a card under the house, with a Menu button and your list always at the top. On older phones that can't show the 3D house, the site switches to a photo version with the same departments and menu.

Questions

Things you might be wondering

Do we keep our web address?

Yes. The new site goes live on homemakersseychelles.com, so existing links, business cards and Google results all lead to it.

Do we have to manage online orders or payments?

No. Customers send a list by email and your team replies with prices and stock, just as you would on the phone. Online payments can be added later if you ever want them.

What if we want something different from the concept?

Tell us on the call. Small changes are free once you buy the site, and anything bigger is quoted before we start, so there are no surprises.

Will it work on older phones?

Yes. Phones that can't show the 3D house get a photo version with the same departments, menu and list.

Who updates it later?

Send us changes over WhatsApp or email. We'll talk through an aftercare option on the call so updates are always quick.
